## 1. Purpose and boundary

This document defines the information boundaries required before A2 Runtime Identity & Access, A3 Customer-to-Financial Account Binding, A4 Capability & Policy Engine, and A5 Internal Financial Pilot. It assigns ownership to identifiers, separates canonical IDs from display or provider references, and defines the handling and retention controls that later phases must implement or formally approve.

This is a current-state consolidation document, not a new runtime contract. It does not introduce an API, data model, migration, deletion job, legal schedule, external integration, or data-subject workflow. A retention duration marked **schedule required** must be approved by the accountable legal, compliance, security, finance, or product owner before production use; the existing operational defaults are recorded without treating them as regulatory advice.

## 2. Governing rules

1. **One canonical owner:** Every identifier is created, validated, and retired by one owning bounded context. A reference to another domain does not transfer ownership.
2. **Customer UUID is the customer anchor:** `Customer.id` is the canonical identifier for customer-owned records. Customer references, aliases, case numbers, and provider references never replace it.
3. **Financial authority is not copied:** Wallet and ledger identifiers identify financial records, but balances, journal state, and monetary truth remain owned by `wallet`/`ledger`. Customer metadata may reference financial records only through an approved binding.
4. **References are not truth:** A display/reference value can locate or correlate a record. It cannot authorize a command, prove ownership, establish settlement, or replace a foreign key.
5. **Scope is explicit:** Uniqueness is declared as global, domain-wide, provider-scoped, owner-scoped, or command-scoped. A bare key is never assumed to be globally unique.
6. **Deletion does not imply reuse:** Soft deletion, retention cleanup, and identifier reuse are separate decisions. Globally unique references and financial identifiers remain unavailable for reuse unless their owner explicitly defines a safe reusable namespace.
7. **Minimize before persisting and sharing:** Store only the fields required for the purpose. Audit, event, metric, and idempotency payloads must not become copies of sensitive source records.
8. **A hold overrides cleanup:** A legal, regulatory, investigation, security, or financial-control hold suspends ordinary deletion or anonymization for all records in its declared scope.
9. **No plaintext security material:** Passwords, tokens, recovery codes, MFA secrets, and device proofs are never stored, logged, audited, or shared in plaintext. A hash is still sensitive data.
10. **Current internal routes are not public:** Identifier visibility does not grant access. Authentication and authorization remain an A2 responsibility, and current internal endpoints require deployment/network restriction.

### 3.1 Current compatibility exception

`wallet_accounts.customer_id` and the legacy `beneficiaries.customer_id` are currently opaque `varchar` values in financial/legacy schemas. The Customer Foundation owns the target customer identity, but these columns are not silently reclassified as canonical UUIDs. A3 must create and reconcile an explicit binding/mapping, preserving the financial record and its history. No document, reference, or display value may be used to rewrite ledger truth.

## 4. Normalization and uniqueness rules

### 4.1 Common pipeline

For a value that participates in lookup or uniqueness:

1. Validate the declared type and maximum length at the boundary.
2. Trim surrounding ASCII whitespace.
3. Apply only the domain-specific normalization in this document. Do not silently transliterate, remove meaningful characters, or apply a new Unicode folding rule to an existing identifier.
4. Validate the normalized form before persistence.
5. Enforce the same rule in the owning service and PostgreSQL constraint/index. A service-only check is not sufficient for concurrent writes.
6. Store the canonical form and return only the minimum representation needed by the caller.

Free-form names, comments, narration, evidence descriptions, and display labels are not identifier namespaces. Trim and length-limit them, but do not lowercase or use them as uniqueness keys unless a separate decision says so.

### 4.2 Canonical rules

- **UUIDs:** Accept UUID syntax case-insensitively at an internal boundary, serialize in lowercase hyphenated form, and never regenerate or recycle an identifier after soft deletion.
- **Safe references:** Customer references, customer-beneficiary references, funding-instrument references, case numbers, wallet aliases, MFA enrollment references, and similar internal references use the current safe form `^[a-z0-9][a-z0-9_.:-]{0,159}$` after trim and lowercase. A reference that is already globally unique remains unavailable after soft deletion.
- **Customer destination identifiers:** The customer-beneficiary module currently creates a comparison value by trimming, lowercasing, and removing whitespace, `(`, `)`, and `-`. The original value and normalized comparison value are separate concerns. Duplicate detection is scoped to one customer and active/non-deleted beneficiaries; the value is still sensitive.
- **Payment references:** Generated by the shared payment registry. They are immutable, not accepted as client-generated IDs, and must remain unique across all payment types that use the registry. A failed or pending payment still owns its reference.
- **Wallet aliases:** Lowercase safe form and active-only global uniqueness as currently defined. Alias reuse after soft deletion must not be mistaken for reuse of a wallet UUID or financial reference.
- **External identifiers:** Do not normalize an external provider value based on MonieNaija conventions. Store a provider, identifier type, and provider value; use a provider-specific canonicalization rule and retain the original only where necessary for evidence.
- **Idempotency keys:** Treat the key as an opaque client token. Trim at the command boundary, preserve case, reject empty or overlong values, and bind it to an explicit command scope. Hash the canonical command payload separately; never use the key as a secret or display value.
- **Request, correlation, and trace IDs:** Accept only bounded printable values at the HTTP boundary, reject control characters, and generate a value when absent. Propagate rather than re-create them inside a module. They have no business uniqueness guarantee.
- **Causation IDs:** Use the parent event/command identity, not a human reference. A missing cause is allowed only for a root command/event; a fabricated cause is a contract error.

## 5. Correlation and traceability map

The correlation chain below links an action to evidence without making the correlation fields authoritative business data.

```text
HTTP request
  requestId + correlationId + traceId
        |
        +--> command scope + idempotency key + request hash
        |
        v
Owning domain transaction
  aggregate/entity UUID + customer UUID where applicable
        |
        +--> immutable audit event
        |      audit ID + entity ID + request ID + correlation ID
        |
        +--> financial journal where applicable
        |      journal ID + ledger IDs + payment reference + correlation ID
        |
        +--> transactional outbox fact
               event ID + aggregate type/ID + event type
               future envelope: correlation ID + causation ID + schema/version
                         |
                         v
              approved consumer/adapter command
                parent causation ID + same workflow correlation ID
                         |
                         v
              callback/settlement/reconciliation evidence
                provider reference + mapped internal record ID
```

| Origin                              | Carries                                                                            | Owner and use                                                                                   | Prohibited interpretation                                                                                          |
| ----------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| HTTP ingress                        | Request, correlation, and trace IDs                                                | Production boundary generates or safely propagates them; Operations uses them to find evidence. | Caller-provided correlation is not proof of caller identity and is not an idempotency claim.                       |
| Retryable command                   | Command scope, idempotency key, request hash                                       | Command owner and Operations deduplicate identical effects.                                     | An idempotency key is not a resource ID, authorization token, or permanent audit identity.                         |
| Customer mutation                   | Customer UUID and owning entity UUID                                               | Customer or adjacent module owns the state and its audit record.                                | A customer reference or alias cannot be used to write another module's tables.                                     |
| Financial command                   | Wallet UUIDs, ledger IDs, payment reference, correlation ID                        | Wallet/Ledger and the financial command owner determine execution and value.                    | A customer-wallet ID, beneficiary reference, or payment reference cannot create or alter a balance.                |
| Domain event                        | Event ID, aggregate identity, schema/version, correlation, and immediate causation | Event owner defines the durable fact; Operations persists the outbox record.                    | An event is not a command, an authorization decision, a ledger posting, or a complete customer record.             |
| External callback or partner result | Provider reference plus mapped internal resource and correlation                   | A future A6 adapter validates the source and reconciles the result.                             | A provider reference is not trusted merely because it matches a local string and cannot replace internal identity. |
| Investigation/reconciliation        | Customer UUID, case number, transaction/payment reference, event/correlation IDs   | Compliance, Finance, Reconciliation, and Operations join evidence read-only.                    | A dashboard or correlation search cannot mutate the source record to make evidence agree.                          |

Current limitation: `OutboxEvent` stores an event UUID, aggregate identity, type, payload, and lifecycle state, but not explicit persisted `correlationId` or `causationId` columns. Before a broker, partner, notification, or external callback is introduced, the versioned event envelope must carry those fields with a minimal payload. This is a future contract input, not an A1T07 schema change.

### 6.1 Hash and device rules

- A hash is an identifier when it can be compared or linked. It is not automatically anonymous, irreversible for every input, or safe to disclose.
- Password, reset-token, recovery-code, MFA, and device-fingerprint hashes remain in the authentication/security boundary. They are not copied into customer profile, audit payload, event payload, analytics, or support exports.
- New security hashes must document algorithm, version, salt/pepper or keyed-hash handling, comparison purpose, rotation, and deletion schedule. The current metadata foundation does not create a runtime authentication or device-proof protocol.
- Logs and audit records should record the action, algorithm/version, and safe outcome—not the raw value or hash—unless a formally approved investigation requires the hash.
- Device identifiers must not become a cross-domain tracking key. Use the authentication-owned device UUID/reference and a purpose-specific comparison value.

### 7.1 Retention and legal-hold controls

- A retention schedule must identify the dataset owner, purpose, start event, cutoff, deletion/anonymization method, access reviewer, evidence required, and exception/hold process.
- A hold record must identify a hold owner, reason/authority, scope (for example Customer UUID, case number, record UUID, payment reference, provider reference, or correlation ID), start date, review date, release authority, and affected datasets.
- Holds expand across the correlation map. A hold on a compliance case or financial dispute may include linked audit, event, idempotency, customer, wallet, ledger, and provider records; the expansion must be recorded rather than inferred ad hoc.
- Hold release is explicit and auditable. Retention cleanup must re-evaluate the ordinary cutoff after release; it must not delete a held record immediately without review.
- Data minimization occurs at collection, mutation, audit/event construction, export, and deletion. An immutable source record is not a reason to keep unnecessary copies in projections or logs.
- A data-subject request must be verified at the future A2 access boundary and evaluated against purpose, legal obligations, financial-record immutability, security evidence, and holds. Do not promise deletion of ledger, audit, compliance, or held records from a generic customer endpoint.
- Existing [`RETENTION-POLICY.md`](RETENTION-POLICY.md) governs only the documented operational defaults until the relevant domain owners approve broader schedules.

## 8. External-sharing restrictions

### 8.1 Default rule

No identifier or classified data is shared outside the authorized MonieNaija boundary by default. External sharing requires a documented purpose, data owner approval, minimum necessary fields, an approved recipient/contract, transport and access controls, retention/deletion terms, and correlation/reconciliation behavior. A future A6 adapter must isolate provider-specific representations from internal canonical IDs.

| Recipient or use                                                             | May share, when approved                                                                                                                                  | Must not share by default                                                                                                                                                                        |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Customer-facing authenticated view (after A2)                                | The customer’s own masked reference, status, selected transaction/payment reference, and minimum necessary display data                                   | Password hashes, tokens, recovery/MFA material, device fingerprints, internal ledger IDs, case details, risk factors, other customers’ data, or unmasked provider identifiers without a purpose. |
| Support or operations                                                        | Minimum fields needed for the assigned task; masked references; request/correlation IDs for evidence lookup; approved payment reference                   | Credentials, raw hashes, full identity documents, unrestricted compliance comments/evidence, full ledger internals, or a broad `SELECT *` export.                                                |
| Compliance, Risk, or Security specialist                                     | Data required by role and case/investigation scope, with access logging and need-to-know review                                                           | Unrelated customer records, reusable credentials, or source data copied into a less restricted system.                                                                                           |
| External KYC, bank, NIBSS, mobile-money, or other provider through future A6 | Provider-specific customer/instrument mapping, minimum identity/account fields, approved provider reference, and required transaction context             | MonieNaija internal UUIDs as the provider’s identity, raw password/MFA/device data, unrelated risk notes, full audit payloads, or unnecessary customer history.                                  |
| Settlement or financial partner                                              | Approved provider reference, payment reference, wallet/account details, amount, currency, status, and reconciliation identifiers required by the contract | Customer authentication data, compliance comments, internal policy reasoning, unrestricted customer profile, or ledger tables.                                                                   |
| Notification or messaging provider through future A7                         | Minimum template variables, masked display values, channel destination under an approved purpose, and notification correlation                            | Credentials, security events, full financial details, risk/compliance content, identity documents, or raw internal IDs where a short-lived provider token suffices.                              |
| Analytics, telemetry, or observability                                       | Aggregated metrics, redacted error codes, purpose-specific pseudonymous IDs, and bounded correlation data                                                 | Raw PII, account numbers, credential/device hashes, full event payloads, case comments, or idempotency keys.                                                                                     |
| Events and outbox consumers                                                  | Versioned minimal fact, owning aggregate ID/reference as authorized, correlation/causation metadata, and source version                                   | Entire source entities, secrets, raw documents, unnecessary financial/identity fields, or a payload that lets a consumer bypass its authority.                                                   |

### 8.2 Identifier-specific restrictions

- Internal UUIDs, ledger IDs, request IDs, trace IDs, correlation IDs, causation IDs, and idempotency keys are not public identifiers. They may be included in a controlled support or provider contract only when their purpose is documented and the value cannot be used to bypass authorization.
- Customer references, wallet aliases, beneficiary references, funding-instrument references, and case numbers are not secrets, but they are linkable and therefore remain Restricted. Mask them in public/support views unless the user or operator is authorized to see the full value.
- Payment references may be shared as transaction references where necessary. They do not authorize a refund, reversal, transfer, or settlement action.
- Provider account numbers, bank codes, mobile-money values, destination identifiers, and device identifiers are shared only under the owner’s approved field-level contract. Do not reuse one provider’s identifier as another provider’s namespace.
- Never send password hashes, token hashes, recovery-code hashes, MFA identifiers, device-fingerprint hashes, identity-document contents, compliance comments, risk-factor reasoning, or unrestricted audit payloads to a general external recipient.
- Callbacks are untrusted input. Validate provider identity, signature/authentication, schema, timestamp/replay controls, provider reference, and mapped internal resource before correlating or changing state.

## 9. ADR-0023 and ADR-0024 input package

### ADR-0023 — Customer Identifier and Reference Conventions

The identifier decision record should ratify:

1. `Customer.id` as the canonical customer UUID and the prohibition on customer references replacing it.
2. Ownership and scope for wallet, ledger, case, beneficiary, funding-instrument, event, correlation, causation, request, trace, and idempotency identifiers.
3. The ASCII-safe normalization rules and the separate treatment of free-form text and provider values.
4. Global, domain, provider, owner, and command-scoped uniqueness, including soft-deletion behavior.
5. The `(scope, key)` idempotency rule, request-hash conflict behavior, and retention interaction.
6. The external-identifier mapping rule: provider values cannot replace internal IDs or financial truth.
7. The future event-envelope requirement for correlation and causation IDs before external publication.

### ADR-0024 — Data Classification and Privacy

The privacy decision record should ratify:

1. The Public/Internal/Confidential/Restricted/Highly Restricted handling levels and stricter legal or contractual overrides.
2. Identity, KYC, risk, compliance, credential, device, beneficiary, funding, financial, operational, and event classification ownership.
3. Minimization and redaction requirements at storage, audit, event, log, export, and external-adapter boundaries.
4. Hash handling, device-fingerprint restrictions, access logging, encryption, and no-plaintext-secret rules.
5. Retention ownership, schedule approval, operational defaults, financial-record immutability, and separation of source-record versus projection retention.
6. Legal-hold scope, correlation-based preservation, release authority, and data-subject request constraints.
7. External-sharing restrictions, field-level approval, provider mapping, callback validation, and partner retention obligations.

These are decision inputs only. Drafting or ratifying ADR-0023/ADR-0024 belongs to the later A1 ADR tasks in the canonical implementation plan.

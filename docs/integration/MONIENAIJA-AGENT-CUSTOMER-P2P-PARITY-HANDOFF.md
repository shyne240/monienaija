## Independent review — what the tests establish

**Cash-to-Cash lock order.** On initiation the funding wallet is write-locked; the existing financial-context/funding authorities lock the funding wallet/ledger/affiliation. The *suspense* selection takes no preliminary SHARE. Ledger locks posting accounts `ORDER BY account.id ASC` with `pessimistic_write`, enforces currency, accounting unit, active status, institutional equality and projected balances. The transaction then revalidates the **same selected account ID** under the institution/scope/purpose/code/active predicates before committing usage and the transfer, funding evidence, locator, audit and outbox. Failed validation rolls the provisional post back. Do not move that check out of the transaction or take the original early SHARE again.

| Competing path | What overlaps and what protects it |
|---|---|
| Initiate / initiate | Different agents can select the same institutional liability. A16's two-arrival barrier tests this exact overlap, distinct immutable locators, no `40P01` **in that characterization**, one journal/key and conserved balances. Closure, a competing row lock and injected late revalidation failure have separate tests. |
| Initiate / claim | `resolveObligationAccountForTransfer` first reads the obligation outside the posting transaction; that lookup is **not** a lock held through settlement. Inside, claim locks the existing transfer, verifies live session/PIN as applicable, funding lines, the unique eligible obligation and institution via `requireClaimChallenge`, consumes the bound OTP and rechecks mutable authority. It holds SHARE locks including institutional accounts before calling the same sorted Ledger writer. C1 concurrently initiates 1,000 and redeems an existing 2,000 on the **same original funding wallet/institution**, once to a customer wallet and once to agent cash float. Each tested pair and its replays leave deltas **−1,000 funder, −1,000 suspense, +2,000 destination**, one journal per financial key. This is one service initiation and one authenticated HTTP claim, not two mobile apps. |
| Concurrent claims / expiry / cancellation | One transfer row serializes terminal transitions; claim rechecks clock expiry, OTP and eligibility before finalizing. Batch expiry uses `FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED`; cancellation locks a still-unclaimed transfer. Expiry/cancellation are status/disposition changes, not a second suspense posting or automatic refund. Neither should adopt initiation's non-locking selection as an authorization shortcut. |
| P2P / agency cash-in | Same operating wallet; bounded funded/overcommit pairs, reservation and journal counts, stable keys on replay. The recorded 12-round stress case stubs the outbox; *different* A23 tests exercise actual transactional outbox/rollback. Do not infer provider delivery from either. |

The fix removes **one demonstrated upgrade cycle**, not all possible cycles. Claim challenge/context SHARE locks and other flows' wallet, usage, account, metrics and evidence locks can still overlap a later UPDATE in a different order. Serialization failure is also possible without a deadlock. Three bounded attempts, retryable driver codes `40001`/`40P01` only; no promise every request finishes under continued contention. The missing historical driver cause cannot be reconstructed from a subsequent trace or green race. Keep redacted attempt/SQLSTATE monitoring, stable-key recovery and a separately approved mixed-load soak; do not increase retries or remove authority locks to make tests pass.

**Separate Cash-Out finding (not Cash-to-Cash).** Two authenticated Wallet→Cash intents share a payer and OTP budget. C1 acquired a SHARE eligibility lock on the customer; one transaction then held the budget and needed UPDATE on that same customer at Ledger's wallet boundary, while the other held SHARE and waited for the budget. In the A14 X diagnostic (real DB, fresh fixtures, original assertions) the server reported **20 `40P01`** deadlocks on `customers`/`cash_out_otp_budgets` across 12 races and **11 `40001`** serialization aborts. Two losers exhausted the budget of three attempts, a third returned “policy unavailable.” This identifies a reproducible A14 mechanism, **not** the absent driver codes from the original A14 failure or historic A16 exhaustion.

Acquiring the final UPDATE eligibility lock *before* the OTP budget eliminated those deadlocks in a 12-race intermediate probe, but one `40001` during policy lookup was caught and mislabeled as an outage. At F, `AgentCashOutService.execute` takes the final lock early; `CustomerFinancialEligibilityService.require` propagates only genuine TypeORM retryable driver errors from policy/evidence reads. The transaction owner may repeat its entire authorization and posting under its **existing** retry bound; the eligibility reader never retries or approves on error. Real missing policy/evidence remains fail-closed. Six unit cases distinguish `40001`/`40P01` from arbitrary errors carrying similar codes. With both changes, **16/16** races resolved to the exact insufficient-funds refusal and one posting, one execution record, debit/credit balances 2,000/8,000. There were zero observed deadlocks and 16 recoverable serialization aborts, not zero contention. Full A14 passed **33/33** separately. **Identity and revocation.** An unbound legacy row may be an address-book hint. It has no historic typed owner proof for the destination. C1 demonstrates that passing its ID to the bound quote endpoint is refused, while a fresh direct preview is possible without it. Newly saved rows bind `(owner, type, id)` immutably; issuance locks/checks the active, verified, owned row and re-resolves the current identifier. Same-key replay of an issued quote returns its immutable facts, and saved-entry suspension blocks **new** bound quotes but does not cancel the previous offer. Changed live session, credential/PIN, participant capability, institution, price, balance, limits or expiry can still refuse execution. Passing a direct wallet ID never grants ownership of the sender. These are specific semantics, not a decision that every legacy destination or outstanding quote is acceptable.

**Contact and delivery.** Passing the endpoint guard once is insufficient for a contact mutation; C1 checks account lock, rotation, deletion and password change **under the write transaction's credential/session locks**. An allocated receiving number is not proof of control for SMS. OTP-backed contact, unambiguous destination, applicable event/channel governance and explicit ordinary-SMS preference are checked. C1 corrects the customer-addressed side of a collision with a verified agent, both at materialization and in the shared pre-send lookup. The permitted synthetic attempt, duplicate suppression, queued opt-out, changed event setting and collision skips are evidence up to the **last local recheck**. `NotificationDeliveryProcessor.markStarted` locks delivery/attempt and event/channel governance, **not** the contact and preference through an external send. A change after lookup can race STARTED/send; once STARTED a transport timeout or lost status write can leave UNKNOWN, not permission for blind resend. Actual SMS/email/push delivery and instantaneous revocation remain unproved.

**Money and commercial evidence.** Compare immutable quote/pricing hash, transfer, principal journal, decisions/registry, exact commercial lines/accounts, direction, unit, currency, totals, VAT split and saved commission base/parameters/tier detail. Fixture example: principal 50,000, fee/VAT 1,000/75 exclusive → payer debit 51,075; inclusive 931/69 → 51,000. Commission can post to payable or an eligible agent wallet depending on configuration; it is not a second subtraction of VAT or a guaranteed entitlement. Tests contradict and restore evidence **only in disposable fixtures**. Missing/unsupported quote or registry/beneficiary evidence produces WARNING where appropriate; contradictions ERROR. A principal-only check or a balanced diverted credit is not commercial acceptance. `COMMERCIAL_LEGS_NOT_RECONCILED` remains intentional for correlated *unquoted* historical commercial legs; no repricing or auto-adjustment of old transactions.

1. EXECUTION-AUTHORITY DEFECT (corrected)
-----------------------------------------
Four scenarios proved that a claim could settle after its authorization had been revoked while
the OTP was being verified:
  - 44. Agent and KYC eligibility rechecked after OTP
  - S2 pin-rotation / pin-lock / pin-deletion committed during OTP verification

Root cause: `AgentCashToCashClaimService.execute()` validated agent cash authority (PIN, class,
capability, session) and customer eligibility *before* opening the settlement transaction, and
verified the OTP *inside* it. Every one of those authorities could change in the window between,
and nothing re-read them before posting.

Correction (single, narrow, inside the existing transaction — no parallel authorization system):
`assertExecutionAuthority(manager, …)` is now called immediately after OTP consumption and before
destination resolution, limit enforcement and posting. It re-reads, under `FOR SHARE`:
  - agent row, credential and session  (reuses `assertAgentSessionWithManager`);
  - agent lifecycle status, agent-class state and `applicable_services` containing CASH_TO_CASH
    (mirrors `AgentServiceCapabilityService` semantics, but scoped to the transaction so it sees
    policy changes committed mid-claim);
  - the Agent PIN row for AGENT_CASH: existence, `deleted_at`, `account_locked`, and exact
    match of `id`/`pin_hash`/`pin_version`/`last_changed_at` against the values captured before
    authorization (a rotation during verification now fails closed);
  - customer financial eligibility (`financialEligibility.require`) and KYC evidence
    (`assertCurrentApprovedKyc` for AGENT_CASH, `assertWalletAssessmentUnexpired` for
    CUSTOMER_WALLET).

Serialization semantics: an authority change that commits *before* this boundary is observed and
the claim is refused with no journal, no wallet credit, no settled transfer and no consumed
obligation. A change that commits *after* a successful claim transaction cannot retroactively
invalidate the settled claim — the revocation serializes behind the posting transaction. The suite
now covers both directions (hook-driven mutations inside the claim transaction, and real
cross-connection updates).

2. LOCATOR LIFECYCLE (corrected — contract, not weakened)
---------------------------------------------------------
The two remaining failures were test-contract errors, not a retirement bug. The governed claim OTP
is single-use and only one challenge may be active for a transfer, so a scenario that issued two
challenges and raced them could never produce a settlement:
  - "number and UUID claims racing": both routes now redeem the SAME transfer-bound challenge, and
    exactly one settlement occurs (the other attempt gets the non-disclosing conflict).
  - "locator settlement failure … retry": the retry now issues its challenge for the Agent/session
    that actually drives the locator route; the locator itself was never retired — the 404 was the
    route masking a 409 binding mismatch as `C2C_CLAIM_NOT_AVAILABLE`.

No locator retirement rule was weakened, no attempt budget was reset, and the number route keeps
returning the non-disclosing `C2C_CLAIM_NOT_AVAILABLE` for client-side failures.
